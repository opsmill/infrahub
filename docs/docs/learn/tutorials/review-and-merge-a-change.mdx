# Review and merge a change

Network automation depends on the data you keep in Infrahub: sites, devices, interfaces, addressing,
and the relationships between them. When that data changes, most organizations want a way to inspect
the proposed change, validate it, and decide whether it should become part of the default state that
everything downstream reads from.

Branches and Proposed Changes are how you do that in Infrahub. You make and inspect your change on a
branch. When it is ready for review, you open a Proposed Change, where a reviewer compares it against
the default branch, reads the validation results, discusses it with you, and approves or rejects it.
Once it is approved, you merge it into the default branch.

In the Quickstart you created the `add-locations` branch and added two sites to it. Here you take
that change through review, validation, approval and merge.

## Before you start

At the end of the Quickstart you have Denver DC1 and Paris DC1 on `add-locations`, and neither of
them on `main`. Those two sites are what you review, approve and merge below. If you are working
from a different branch, substitute its name wherever `add-locations` appears.

You need an Infrahub instance you can sign in to, and a branch with at least one change on it that
is not on the default branch. A connected Git repository is not required.

Step 4 covers the approval. Infrahub does not accept a review from the person who opened the Proposed
Change, so on a single-user instance that step is optional — you can create a second account to carry
it out, or read what a reviewer would do and continue to the merge.

## Step 1: Review your change

Read the change yourself first. The diff shows what your branch adds, removes and modifies against
the default branch, and it is the same view a reviewer opens.

1. Select **Branches** in the left menu.
2. Select `add-locations`.
3. Open the **Data** tab.

Denver DC1 and Paris DC1 are listed as additions. Expand either one for the attribute values your
change sets, and confirm they are what you meant to propose.

## Step 2: Request review of the change

Your change is on the branch. Open a Proposed Change so that a reviewer can read it. It holds the
proposed differences, the validation results, the discussion and the review decision, and you merge
from it once a reviewer approves.

1. Select **Propose change** on the branch page. The form opens with `add-locations` already set as
   the source branch.
2. Select `main` as the destination branch.
3. Name it `Add Denver and Paris sites`.
4. Write a description. The diff already lists what changed, so use this for why you are changing it:
   who asked, the site that is opening, the ticket it closes. Your reviewer reads this first.
5. Add your reviewers.
6. Open the Proposed Change.

Open it as a draft instead when your change is not finished. Infrahub runs the same checks either
way, and a draft tells your reviewers you are not asking for a decision yet.

## Step 3: Review the validation results

Before approving a change — or before asking a reviewer to — read its validation results. They
identify conditions that have to be resolved before the change can be merged.

Open the **Checks** tab.

Infrahub runs a set of built-in checks whenever you open a Proposed Change, with nothing for you to
configure:

- **Data integrity** — whether the branch is still consistent with the default branch, and whether
  every reference between objects resolves.
- **Merge conflict detection** — attributes that changed on both branches, which Infrahub cannot
  decide between on its own.
- **Schema validation** — any change to the data model.
- **Git repository integration** — conflicts in a connected repository. With no repository
  connected, this reports nothing.

A failing check prevents the merge, so investigate any failure before going further.

Organizations add checks of their own for rules Infrahub cannot know about — a device name that has
to match a naming convention, or a redundant WAN circuit that has to be in operational state at every
site. Write them in Python, store them in a Git repository connected to Infrahub, and they run here
alongside the built-in ones.
[Build a check](./build-a-check) walks through writing one.

## Step 4: Review and approve the change

In a team, this step belongs to a reviewer. They open the Proposed Change, read the
proposed differences, read the validation results, ask whatever they need to ask, and record a
decision: **Approve** when the change is ready to merge, **Reject** when it needs correcting first.
They can attach a comment to one part of the diff rather than to the change as a whole, which keeps a
question about a single attribute next to that attribute.

Infrahub does not accept a review from the person who opened the Proposed Change. That is the reason
the workflow exists: the change is read by a reviewer who did not write it.

On a single-user instance you are that person, so you have two ways to continue.

**To see the approval happen**, create a second account using
[Manage accounts and permissions](../../deploy-manage/user-management/permissions-roles/manage-accounts-and-permissions#creating-a-new-account),
sign in as it, open the Proposed Change and select **Approve**. Then sign back in as your own
account for step 5.

**To carry on without it**, go straight to step 5. On Community an approval is not required before a
merge, so the change merges either way. You will not see the approval recorded, and the rest of the
tutorial works the same.

However the approval is given, Infrahub records who gave it and when. Approving does not merge the
change: merging is a separate action, so the person who approves and the person who merges can be
different people.

:::note Enterprise
Enterprise turns the review step into a rule rather than a convention: require a number of approvals
before anyone can merge, and withdraw an approval automatically when the branch changes after that
approval was given. See
[Implement a change approval workflow](../../change-approval/change-approval-workflow) for
configuring those policies.
:::

## Step 5: Merge the approved change

Once the change has been through review, merge it so the approved values become part of the default
state.

1. Select **Merge** on the Proposed Change.
2. Switch the branch selector back to `main`.

Denver DC1 and Paris DC1 are on `main`, where the rest of your automation reads them.
`add-locations` becomes read-only and stays available for reference, unless your instance is
configured to delete merged branches. [Merge a branch](../../branches/merge) covers what Infrahub
does during a merge in more detail.

## What you learned

That is the review most organizations require before data reaches the default branch:

**Change separately.** You made the change on a branch rather than directly on `main`.

**Understand what will change.** You read the difference between the branch and the default branch
before asking a reviewer to read it.

**Validate it.** You read the automated checks before deciding whether the change was ready.

**Review and approve it.** A reviewer examines the change and records a decision. Infrahub does not
accept a review from the author, so this step always belongs to a reviewer.

**Publish the approved state.** You merged the reviewed change into `main`, where it becomes input to
the rest of your automation.

## Not covered here

**Rebasing.** When `main` changes while your Proposed Change is open, your branch started
from an older version of it and you rebase before you merge. See
[Rebase a branch](../../branches/rebase).

**Conflicts.** When the same attribute changed on both branches, Infrahub stops the merge and asks
you which value to keep. See [Resolve conflicts](../../branches/resolve-conflicts) and
[Resolve a proposed-change conflict](../../proposed-changes/resolve-conflict).
